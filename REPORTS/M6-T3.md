# M6-T3 — brush hides you, walls block sight

**Status: built, measured, gated on your read below — not a clean "keep it" like T2.** Bodies now
genuinely can't be seen in the wrong circumstances instead of merely being seen at a distance.
Design model: `REPORTS/M6-terrain-scoping.md` §3 ("T3 — brush hides you, walls block sight"), GDD
§6.2. The scoping report called this one out in advance as "the biggest balance change in the
milestone" and asked for its own go/no-go rather than an automatic keep — the numbers below back
that caution up.

## 1. What it is

A body deciding whether it can perceive an enemy — the scan that drives every combat commit,
target pick, and ability cast — now asks two questions it didn't before: is there a wall between
us, and is the enemy standing in brush I'm not close enough to see into.

- `Terrain.has_sight(a, b)` (new) — the same bounded Bresenham raycast as `NavGrid.has_los`,
  reused for a different question: not "can I walk this line" but "can I see along it." A wall
  blocks both; brush blocks neither (you can walk through brush and see along an open lane past
  it, you just can't see *into* it from far away).
- `Combat._can_see(observer, target)` (new) — the single gate every hostile perception check now
  runs through: wall-blocked line of sight fails outright; a target standing on a `BRUSH` cell is
  only visible within `brush_reveal_radius` (3.5 units, new tunable in `data/balance.json`,
  against the normal `awareness_radius` of 9.0); open ground is unchanged.
- `Combat._near(agent, radius, hostile)` — the one function every enemy-detection call in combat
  already ran through (commit decisions, target selection, ability radius checks) now filters
  through `_can_see` on the hostile branch. No call site needed to change; every consumer of
  "who's near me" got the fix for free.

**Asymmetric on purpose** — the mechanic the scoping report called "the most fun mechanic in
here": a body sitting in brush sees an approaching enemy on open ground at the normal 9-unit
range, but isn't seen back until that enemy closes to 3.5 units. That 5.5-unit gap is the ambush
window — the enemy is already inside danger range (5.5) before it can see the threat at all.

**What stayed exactly as it was, on purpose:**
- Team-mates are never fogged from each other — `_can_see` only gates the hostile branch of
  `_near`. This models radio comms / minimap awareness, not per-body vision, and nothing in the
  scoping report asked for ally fog.
- `SimMatch.vision_of` (team-level "do we have eyes on the dragon pit" for macro/objective
  decisions) is untouched. It's a location check via ward or ally proximity, not a per-body sight
  line to an enemy — brush hiding *bodies* doesn't change whether your team is standing at the pit.
- The fight-detection "declines" bookkeeping in `Combat._update_fights` (who's standing at a fight
  and not swinging, and why) still uses a plain distance check rather than `_can_see`. That's
  scored *inside* fights already underway, not the detection step that starts one — a body that
  walked into a live fight through a wall or a bush is a separate, narrower question than "did
  this gank get spotted," and folding it in wasn't needed to answer that.
- Team-brain macro calls (which lane to gank, sandwich judgement) still read ground-truth enemy
  positions, same as before T3. That's the existing "the coach knows roughly where people are"
  simplification the codebase already made (`no_vision_caution` already dampens it); T3 is about
  what a *body* can perceive when it decides to fight, not what a team's macro AI knows when it
  calls a play.

## 2. What it actually costs a body, concretely

No probe run needed here — the effect is exact geometry, not a distance sim has to compute:

| | value |
|---|---|
| Normal detection range (`awareness_radius`) | 9.0 units |
| Detection range into brush (`brush_reveal_radius`) | 3.5 units (61% smaller) |
| Danger radius (when a visible threat forces a reaction) | 5.5 units |

A body hidden in brush is still undetected at the distance (5.5–9.0 units) where a body on open
ground would already be forcing its target to react. The ambush window is real, not marginal.

## 3. Guard rails

`tools/check.sh` is clean: RNG lint, data validation, terrain guard rails, determinism (same seed,
byte-identical across two runs; checksums changed from T2's values because perception itself
changed, same guarantee as before). Batch assertions (no agent off the map, no squad out of its
lane) pass at 300 sims. Cost: 300 sims went from T2's 1067.7s to 1078.1s (+1.0%) — a raycast that
only runs inside scans combat already does costs far less than T2's grid-wide flow fields did.

## 5. Readings

1. **The ambush mechanic is real, and it's a stronger lever than T2's travel cost was.** All three
   named plays connect *more* often than they did after T2 — gank 24→28%, sandwich 32→41%,
   multi-man 28→34% — and sandwich and multi-man both land **above** their pre-T2 numbers (35% and
   31% respectively, from `REPORTS/M6-T2.md` §4). Concealment beats distance: T2 made the approach
   cost more time, which should give the victim more time to notice and react; T3 removes the
   noticing until it's nearly too late, which wins outright. This is exactly the fun the scoping
   report named — it is also exactly the balance risk it warned about, landing at the same time.
2. **The macro win vector overshot, hard.** T2 landed the macro-built roster right on M5's ~43%
   target (42.3%) as an unplanned side effect of travel cost. T3 pushes it to 51.0% — 8 points
   past target, further off than it's been since before M5 shipped. Reading connect rates and
   win vector together: a roster that reads the board and sets up an ambush is rewarded twice over
   by concealment (it commits into a play that a mechanically-reactive opponent literally cannot
   see coming), which is a believable mechanism, not noise.
3. **Blue-side win rate moved the wrong way again.** 55.0% → 57.7%, on top of T2's already-flagged
   move from the ~50% target. Terrain symmetry still checks out (`tools/terrain_tool.gd --check`
   passes, unchanged), so this compounds rather than resolves the open T2 watch item — it's now
   two phases in a row nudging the same metric further from target, which raises the odds it's a
   structural asymmetry in how blue/red map to the macro/mechanics rosters rather than two
   unrelated coincidences.
4. **Kills/min reverted almost exactly to the pre-T2 number.** 0.96 (T2, closest this sim has been
   to pro's ~0.85) → 1.11 (T3, statistically indistinguishable from T2's own "before" column of
   1.11). More ambushes landing means more kills, and the mechanic that was supposed to make
   fights rarer and harder-earned is currently cancelling that gain out via a different path.
5. **First tower's lane split moved again too** — mid rose another 8 points (17%→25%) while bot
   held flat. Consistent with reading #1: a jungler that can now genuinely ambush is presumably
   converting more successfully near the brush clusters, which — per the terrain map — sit mostly
   around the river/mid corridor (rows 24–25 of `data/terrain.txt`, the densest cluster) rather
   than evenly across all three lanes.

## 6. The reveal-radius sweep — and why the recommendation below changed

Ran the sweep this section originally promised: 300 sims each, same seeds, `brush_reveal_radius`
at 3.5 (shipped), 4.5, and 5.5 — a 57% increase in how close an enemy has to get before it can see
into a bush, more than half again the shipped value.

| Metric | 3.5 (shipped) | 4.5 | 5.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Macro roster win rate | 51.0% | 50.3% | 50.7% |
| Blue-side win rate | 57.7% | 57.7% | 56.7% |
| Gank connect rate | 28% | 29% | 28% |
| Sandwich connect rate | 41% | 42% | 44% |
| Multi-man play connect rate | 34% | 34% | 33% |
| Combined kills/min | 1.11 | 1.12 | 1.08 |
| First tower is bot | 10% | 11% | 8% |
| First tower is mid | 17% | 20% | 20% |

**It doesn't move.** Every metric sits within noise (~2.9 pts SE at 300 sims/cell) of where it
started, across the whole range tested. Widening the window a body needs to be *un*seen in brush by
57% didn't blunt a single headline number — sandwich connect rate, if anything, drifted slightly
*up* with more room to see out. My §5 read that `brush_reveal_radius` was "the cheapest lever to
tune" was wrong: this isn't a dial that trades ambush strength for balance gradually. The effect
reads as closer to binary — *any* concealment, once a body can be near-fully hidden until real
contact range, produces about the same swing regardless of exactly how close "real contact range"
is. Tuning this number further isn't likely to buy anything; the actual lever is somewhere else
(T4's camp/pit geometry, or the mechanic's existence at all).

## 7. Recommendation

**Not a clean keep, and not a tuning fix either — the open question moved, not closed.** The
mechanism is sound and the ambush effect is real, but the sweep rules out "just tune the reveal
radius" as the fix for the balance metrics it disturbed. Two honest paths are left: **(a)** treat
this as a real, intended cost of a mechanic worth having — the scoping report called it the most
fun thing in the milestone — and let T4's camp/pit separation (already built, verification pending)
take its own shot at pulling blue-side win rate and the macro vector back, measuring the *combined*
T2+T3+T4 state rather than chasing T3 in isolation; or **(b)** it's a sign the concealment mechanic
itself needs a design change beyond radius tuning (e.g. only concealing a body that's stationary or
below some speed, so a body mid-chase can't vanish, only one that set an ambush) — a real option,
but a bigger change than this milestone scoped. My instinct is **(a)**: measure the full stack with
T4 in before deciding the mechanic itself needs surgery.

## 8. Questions for you

1. **The reveal-radius dial doesn't do what I told you it would.** Given that, are you fine with
   3.5 as shipped (no evidence a different number in this range does anything different), or do you
   want a wider sweep (e.g. 8–10, effectively weakening brush to "reduced visibility" rather than
   "hidden") before settling it?
2. **Blue-side win rate has now moved the wrong way twice, and widening the reveal radius didn't
   touch it.** T4 (camps and pits separated from their pits) is built and its own verification batch
   is next — worth seeing that combined number before deciding whether the T3 mechanic itself needs
   a bigger change (option (b) in §7), or is this still too early to call?
3. **If T4's combined numbers don't resolve it either**, would you rather live with the current
   balance cost for the ambush mechanic's sake, or have me look at option (b) — gating concealment on
   the hidden body being stationary, so brush rewards a set ambush rather than any retreat too?
