# M4.5 — Sim depth: space, health, agency — Report

## Why this milestone existed
The M4 viewer was readable but the players didn't behave like players. Two designer
playtests produced a long list of problems that all traced to one root cause: **map positions
were decorative**. The sim computed outcomes from aggregate power scores, so "all the points
merge and suddenly we see death", minions appeared at the front, players walked off the map,
chases ran forever, and turrets did nothing. The fix was to make space, health and intent
*real* — the design truth is in [GDD §6.1](../GDD.md).

This was done in seven phases (A–G), each committed and left runnable.

## What changed, phase by phase
- **A — Foundations.** Live HP on every player (regen on the map, fast in fountain, low-HP
  recall), per-character combat stats (`attack_range`, `preferred_range`, `attack_speed`,
  `fight_role`), real HP bars, minion dots, XP retune.
- **B — Spatial combat engine.** `Combat.resolve()` — the single power-score comparison — is
  gone. Every tick each player perceives nearby enemies, decides whether to commit, picks a
  target, steers to the range its character wants to fight at, and swings; **death happens at
  0 HP**, never from a lottery. Ultimates fire on conditions. A fight detector clusters
  engagements so the kill feed still narrates. Two rules became GDD truth: reach is paid for
  in damage (melee hit harder), and contest an objective only if you can win it.
- **C — Bounds, towers, ending chases.** The map has edges (every position write clamps to
  the playable area). Towers shoot, with LoL aggro, and can kill — credited to the diver who
  forced it or to nobody. Chases end three ways (out of reach too long, dragged past the
  leash, or diving a tower you can't afford). Teams spread targets so five players stop
  chasing one runner. The outer towers moved to lane param 0.30/0.70 (they had sat so close to
  lane centre that turret range covered the whole laning zone).
- **D — Minions march.** Waves stopped teleporting to the front. A lane is now a handful of
  marching **squads** that spawn at base, walk down the lane, meet in the middle and grind
  each other down; they never leave their lane. `front_t` became *derived* from where the
  squads meet.
- **E — Lane agency.** Laners pick a stance (push / trade / all-in / freeze / back) and
  **trade HP through poke**. Poke is only chip pressure (floored high enough it can't tip an
  even lane into a fight); a **solo kill** needs a real edge — a level/item lead, or a gank's
  third body — and is dealt by the combat engine. Top and mid are on the kill board now, not
  just bot.
- **F — Intent, perception, reaction.** `TeamBrain` gained a **blackboard**: a jungler's gank
  posts a call, and *who follows up is rolled from each laner's `macro`* — so the same gank
  reads as coordinated or not depending on who you fielded (macro made visible, Pillar 1). A
  shoved-in mid roams to the call. Objective decisions are **vision-gated** — a team that
  can't see the pit judges the fight on worse information, so a ward there is worth placing.
- **G — Rebalance + behavioural metrics.** The batch runner ([tools/batch_run.gd](../tools/batch_run.gd))
  now reports *behaviour*, not just outcomes — where kills happen, solo vs assisted, before vs
  after 14 min, fight count and duration, gank coordination, mid-game level — and **asserts**
  the bugs the designer had to catch by eye (no agent off the map, no squad out of its lane).
  Then the game was retuned to targets.

## The tuning story (numbers, not guesses)
M3 passed on outcome metrics (33 kills, 32 min, 66% conversion) while the behaviour underneath
was broken. So G measured behaviour first, then tuned. The story:

1. **After B–F the game ran short and snowbally** — ~19 min, ~12 kills, 50% of them in
   laning, gold-leader-at-15 winning only ~50%. Advantage had nowhere to land because sieging
   and lane pressure barely existed; then C–F gave it somewhere (towers, dives, solo kills,
   coordinated ganks) and it swung the other way.
2. **Length**: games ended by lane-pushing before teams grouped and fought. Raising tower HP
   (2300→3400) and nexus HP (4200→6000) gave the close more time — length rose to ~25 min,
   fights per match doubled, kills climbed, and the kill share in laning fell from 50% to ~22%
   (most kills are teamfights again). Baron (spawns at 20 min) started to matter.
3. **First blood** at ~3 min was laning tipping into fights too early; raising the lane commit
   margin (1.65→1.95) pushed it to ~4–5 min.
4. **Snowball**: the longer close let the ahead team convert too hard (~72%). Softening the
   gold→power curve (`power_item_divisor` 6000→7800) and raising shutdown bounties
   (120→160) flattened it back toward the target and let behind teams survive to fight.

## Behavioural check (the thing M3 didn't measure)
M3 hit its outcome targets while the behaviour underneath was broken. These are the numbers
that would have caught it — all healthy now:

|---|---|---|

## Two balance items left for the designer's call
Both trace to one tension and are genuinely a design question, not a missed dial:

- **Gold-lead conversion 70.8% (target ~65%)** and **the Wolves/Ravens split at 30/70**
  (M3 was a healthier 43/57). The mechanics team (Ravens) wins fights on raw mechanics and
  snowballs; softening mechanics (done, to `mechanics_power_divisor` 950) helped the split but
  raised conversion, because it left *gold* as the only thing deciding games. Fight variance
  (raised to 0.20) opened comeback paths and pulled conversion back from 76% to 71%.
- The clean fix for **both at once** is to give **macro its own win vector** — a set-piece
  bonus so a coordinated team wins objective and defensive fights even when behind on gold,
  exactly the lever M3 used. That is a feature (a real fight modifier from the blackboard /
  objective state), not a number, so it is flagged here for a decision rather than guessed at.
  A designer playtest should say whether the current asymmetry (mechanics clearly stronger)
  is the intended feel or wants correcting.

## What to look at in the viewer
A person watching at 1x should be able to narrate the game (the CLAUDE.md "watchable" bar):
- Minions **walk out of base** down their lane and collide in the middle.
- A chase **ends** — pursuers peel off at the tower line — and someone **dies to a turret** on
  a dive (feed says `TURRET`).
- A **solo kill in top or mid**, not just bot.
- A jungler ganks and the feed says **"gank bot (2 follow)"** or **"(no follow-up)"** — the
  roster's macro deciding whether it was coordinated.

## Open design questions (need a designer answer)
1. **Is the mechanics-over-macro asymmetry (30/70) the intended feel, or should macro win
   closer to 43/57?** If the latter, the next step is a macro fight-bonus (see above) rather
   than more dial-turning.
2. **Is a 70% conversion of a 15-min gold lead "leads matter" enough, or too snowbally?** It
   is ~6 points above the old target; lowering it means more comeback variance, which trades
   against readability.
3. **Ability kits** remain parked (slow / dash / stun / shield on short cooldowns). Chases are
   fixed mechanically now, but kits are the main lever left for *fight excitement* — worth
   scheduling before M7 polish?
