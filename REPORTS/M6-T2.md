# M6-T2 — movement respects walls

**Status: shipped, pending your read of the numbers below.** Bodies now route around the terrain
`data/terrain.txt` draws instead of walking through it. Design model: `REPORTS/M6-terrain-scoping.md`
§3 ("T2 — movement respects walls"), GDD §6.2.

## 1. What it is

A body's FSM-level walks — to a lane, to a camp, on a gank, to a rally point, on the one committed
multi-man play — now check whether the straight line to where they're going crosses a wall, and
route around it when it does. Combat's own steering (a fight's stand position) is untouched on
purpose: a fight happens in the open pocket it's already standing in, not through rock
(`REPORTS/M6-terrain-scoping.md` §4).

**The mechanism, precomputed once, not searched per agent or per tick** — the determinism rule in
CLAUDE.md, applied to movement:

- `sim/nav_grid.gd` (new) — `NavGrid`. For every destination a body actually walks to (a "hub"), a
  multi-source BFS flow field over the 50×50 grid: every walkable cell gets a hop-distance and a
  direction to step to shrink it by one. Thirteen hubs today: the three lanes (seeded from every
  cell their polyline crosses, so "walk to top" routes to the *nearest point on that lane*, not one
  fixed spot), the two objective pits, and the eight jungle camps.
- `SimMap.build_nav()` assembles the hub seed sets from geometry `SimMap` already owns and hands
  them to `NavGrid`. Called once, from `DataLoader.load_all`, right after terrain's own guard rails
  pass — so a broken map still fails the way it always has, before nav is ever touched — and the
  resulting `NavGrid` is shared by every match built from that data, batch or otherwise, the same
  way `characters`/`balance` already are.
- `PlayerAgent._step_toward` is the only thing that changed at the movement layer: straight line if
  it's clear (`NavGrid.has_los`, a bounded grid raycast — most of the map, most of the time), the
  next flow-field step if it isn't. Never a stall: an unknown hub or a field with nothing left to
  say both fall back to the straight line, same as before T2 existed.
- One destination has no hub of its own: a committed multi-man play's target is a live enemy
  position (`TeamBrain._punish_target`). `NavGrid.nearest_hub` picks the closest of the thirteen by
  a straight-line scan, done once when the play is called (`TeamBrain._consider_play`, every few
  seconds per team), not per tick.

Every other walk (`TO_LANE`/`FARMING` stand positions, `TO_CAMP`, `GANKING`'s cut-off point or lane
fallback, `GROUPING`/`HOLDING`'s rally) already sits exactly on a lane, camp, or pit, so the hub is
just named at the call site — no runtime lookup needed for those at all.

**What stayed exactly as it was, on purpose** (`REPORTS/M6-terrain-scoping.md` §4): no per-body
collision, no minions leaving their lanes, recalls and deaths still teleport straight to base — none
of that is a walk.

## 2. What it actually costs a body, concretely

A throwaway probe (not shipped) walked five representative trips through the real flow fields:

| Trip | straight line | line clear? | routed distance |
|---|---|---|---|
| blue base → `b_camp_bot` | 56.5 | no | 63.0 (+11%) |
| blue base → dragon pit | 61.8 | no | 76.0 (+23%) |
| blue base → top lane (mid-lane) | 69.0 | yes | 69.0 (+0%) |
| `r_camp_top` → mid lane | 21.4 | yes | 21.0 (+0%) |
| blue jungle pocket → bot lane | 72.2 | no | 95.0 (+32%) |

Open ground costs nothing extra, as designed. A cross-map or jungle-crossing trip now costs real
extra distance — 11–32% here — which is exactly the lever the scoping report named: ganks arrive
later, rotations cost more, a chokepoint is now a chokepoint.

## 3. Guard rails

`tools/check.sh` is clean: RNG lint, data validation, terrain guard rails, and — the one that
matters here — **determinism holds**. Same seed still produces byte-identical output across two
runs (checksums for seeds 1/42/987654321 all changed *from* their pre-T2 values, because movement
itself changed, but stayed internally reproducible, which is the actual guarantee). Batch assertions
(no agent off the map, no squad out of its lane) pass at 300 sims. Cost: 300 sims went from 982.9s to
1067.7s (+8.6%) — cheap enough that batch tuning is unaffected.

## 5. Readings

1. **The macro win vector moved onto target, and T2 wasn't aimed at it.** The macro-built roster
   (azure_wolves, avg macro 82.6 vs. the other roster's 68.0) went from 32.3% to 42.3% — right onto
   M5's ~43% target — without anything in T2 touching the macro gates or comp math M5 tuned. The
   read: once a rotation costs real time, the roster that reads the board and commits early gets
   rewarded relative to the one that reacts on pure mechanics. That's the kind of side effect that's
   worth telling you about rather than burying — it's good news, but it's good news T2 didn't intend,
   which means it's also fragile to whatever T3/T4 do next.
2. **Connect rates fell exactly as predicted, on all three named plays.** Gank (30→24%), sandwich
   (35→32%) and multi-man (31→28%) all drop 3–6 points. A jungler or a collapsing team now has to
   cross the same terrain the fleeing player does, and the fleer benefits more than the chaser under
   the current camp/lane layout — which is a real, not obviously wrong, consequence of the geometry,
   but it's the connect-rate line I'd re-check once M6-T4 moves the camps that currently sit inside
   the dragon/baron pits (`REPORTS/M6-terrain-scoping.md` §2 finding 1) into their real pockets:
   jungle routing near bot/dragon is exactly where that overlap lives today.
3. **Kills/min moved toward pro, from the wrong side to the right side.** 1.11 was overshooting
   pro's ~0.85; 0.96 is now the closest this sim has been to that number without deliberately tuning
   for it (fewer, harder-earned kills is what real chokepoints buy).
4. **Two items worth your eyes before calling T2 fully closed:**
   - **Blue-side win rate moved 49.7% → 55.0%**, more than one standard error. Terrain's own guard
     rail still confirms strict 180°-rotational symmetry (`tools/terrain_tool.gd --check` passes),
     so this isn't a drawn-map asymmetry — it's some interaction between real travel cost and how
     blue/red get assigned to the macro/mechanics rosters across the batch. Not blocking, but worth
     a bigger sample (or a re-read at T4 sign-off) before it's called settled.
   - **First tower's lane split shifted** — bot dropped 17%→10%, mid rose 9%→17% — while the average
     timing barely moved (8.4→8.6 min). I don't have a confirmed cause; my best guess is the same
     bot/dragon camp-overlap named above distorting jungler presence near bot lane. Flagging rather
     than explaining away.

## 6. Recommendation

**Keep it.** Determinism and every assertion hold, the cost is cheap, and the headline numbers move
the right direction — kills/min toward pro, and the macro win-vector target essentially met as a
side effect. The two watch items in §5.4 are real but not disqualifying, and both point at the same
known, already-scheduled fix: **M6-T4's camp/pit repositioning**. My instinct is to run T3 (brush and
vision) next as planned, then re-measure the full batch at T4 once the camps move — at which point
blue-side win rate and the tower-lane split either resolve on their own or turn into a named T4 item.
