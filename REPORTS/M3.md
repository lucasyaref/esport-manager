# M3 — Sim core: combat, ganks, objectives, fights, win — Report

## What changed
The sim now plays complete matches — from first wave to a nexus falling — with everything in between: ganks, skirmishes, teamfights, ultimates, Dragon/Baron, towers, and a real win condition. Still fully deterministic (same seed ⇒ byte-identical), still headless-batchable.

New sim modules (all pure GDScript, no scene deps):
- **`sim/combat.gd`** — one fight resolver for every scale (gank, skirmish, teamfight, objective/siege contest). Each participant's fight power = character stats at level × power curve (early/late) × item factor (gold spent) × mechanics × comp modifiers, plus ultimate impact when off cooldown, team buffs (Dragon/Baron), a numbers advantage for the bigger group, and seeded variance. The stronger side wins; deaths scale with how lopsided the fight was; kills pay gold (with shutdown bounties on fed players), assists, and XP; the dead start respawn timers.
- **`sim/objectives.gd`** — towers (3 tiers/lane) and nexus with HP, sieged by minions + present players (Baron buff multiplies siege). Dragon (stacking permanent team buff) and Baron (big temporary buff) spawn on timers; a contested pit becomes a teamfight, an uncontested one is channeled and taken. Owns the win condition.
- **`sim/team_brain.gd`** — per-team macro AI. After laning, teams decide to group for an objective, siege a pushed lane, defend, or keep farming. Decision quality scales with the roster's **Macro** attribute — low-macro teams rotate later. Split-pushers (global-teleport ult) keep pressuring side lanes instead of grouping.
- **`sim/player_agent.gd`** (extended) — full combat life-cycle: death/respawn, ultimates (unlock lvl 6, run on cooldown), jungler gank decisions, grouping/holding for team play, support warding (wards cut enemy gank success nearby).

All combat/objective/gank numbers live in [data/balance.json](../data/balance.json) (new `combat`, `ganks`, `wards`, `towers`, `objectives` sections), documented for tuning in [data/README.md](../data/README.md).

## Design decision honored: comeback-friendly, but leads matter
Per your call, snowball is tuned so a gold lead raises win probability through item/level power (roughly linear — no runaway), while comeback paths stay open: **shutdown bounties** make a fed player worth far more when they finally die, dying players are worth less (no feeding jackpot), and fight variance lets a behind-but-not-broken team win a decisive objective fight. No artificial rubber-banding — losing earns no free gold. The batch measures this directly (gold-leader-at-15-min win rate — see below).

## The tuning story (numbers, not guesses)
Balancing was done by repeated headless batch runs, watching four dials:
1. **Side balance** (blue vs red) — must be ~50%, or the map geometry is unfair.
2. **Team matchup** (macro Wolves vs mechanics Ravens) — the two test teams are asymmetric by design; the question was whether *either playstyle inherently dominates*.
3. **Match length & kills** — must read like a real game (25–35 min, plausible kill counts).
4. **Snowball** — leads matter but comebacks happen.

The first batches exposed that **raw mechanics dominated macro** (Ravens won ~73–94%). The fix was principled, not a thumb on the scale for two specific teams: I gave **Macro** real systemic value — a set-piece bonus so macro teams win objective and organized-defense fights on their terms — and softened per-fight mechanics dominance, while raising shutdown bounties as a comeback lever. The result is a **healthy asymmetry**: the mechanically superior Ravens keep a real but modest edge (57%), and the macro Wolves win 43% — a matchup, not a formality. The two win differently, too: Ravens through kills and ganks, Wolves through objectives and set-piece fights.

## What to look at in-game
Press Play: the screen simulates a full match twice (determinism check), then shows the winner, the final scoreboard with K/D/A per player, and a kill feed. Run different seeds via `tools/headless_run.gd -- --seed=N` to watch different games; `tools/batch_run.gd -- --sims=N` regenerates the validation numbers.

## Open design questions
1. **Snowball strength**: at 66% leader-win this matches real-LoL feel — leads matter, comebacks live. If after watching M4 you want leads to bite harder or softer, it's one dial (`power_item_divisor`). No action needed now.
2. **Team matchup 57/43**: is a modest mechanics-team edge the right feel, or should the two test teams be closer? Trivial to shift (the set-piece macro bonus), but I'd wait until you can watch them in M4 before deciding.
3. Combat *feel* generally is best judged watching the M4 viewer, not from numbers.
