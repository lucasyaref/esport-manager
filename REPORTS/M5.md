# M5 — Macro play: cross-map coordination — sign-off

**Status: done.** Phases A–F shipped the plays; phase G is the balance pass, the batch sign-off and
this report. Per-phase rationale and numbers are in `CHANGELOG.md`; `REPORTS/M5-C.md`, `M5-D.md`,
`M5-E.md`, `M5-F1.md` and `M5-F2.md` hold the phase reports.

## 1. What M5 was for

Two things at once. The designer's M4.5 playtest said bots stay lane-bound and passive, with no
pro-style coordinated cross-map plays. And the same absence was the **macro win-vector**: two
rosters, one built on `macro` and one on mechanics, and the win split sat far below the ~43/57
target because macro bought almost nothing.

Both are now closed.

200 sims per cell, so the standard error on a win rate is ~3.4 points and ~2.4 on the combined.

**The win-vector target is met** — 42.5% against ~43%, from 34.5% before M5-F2. Macro now buys
something: the committed multi-man play (M5-F2, +4 points) is the single largest contributor, and

but it is packed into a shorter game, so the two misses are the same miss).

## 3. What phase G actually did

### 3.1 The instrument came first (M6-A, shipped alongside)

`sim/highlights.gd` scores a finished match into the moments worth watching. It is a pure read of
the event stream, so it was free to build now and it doubles as a balance metric. Full write-up in
`REPORTS/M6-A.md`. Building it forced `peak` and `present` onto `fight_end`, and that is what made
the rest of this phase possible: for the first time the sim could be asked *how big fights get and
why*.

The answer overturned the assumption phase G started from. The M6 scoping report's "no fight has
ever reached six participants" was measured before M5-E/F and **is no longer true** — the average
match's biggest fight is 5.7 bodies. But fights are still overwhelmingly small (65% are two
bodies), and the reason is not macro:

> At the average fight, **3.9 bodies stand within reach of an enemy and only 2.6 swing.**

The teams *do* converge. The missing bodies are there and declining. So `fight_end` also carries
*why* each one declined, and phase G went after the reasons instead of adding more plays.

### 3.2 The reinforcement was locked out of the fight it walked into — fixed

**69% of "standing at a fight and not swinging" was the disengage lock.**

That lock is hysteresis for *breaking off*: someone who was fighting and pulled out stays out for a
few seconds instead of bouncing back in every tick. It was being applied on **every** decline,
including "I have not arrived yet". A third man walking into a 1v1 sees the two enemies before his
own ally enters his awareness radius, reads the local numbers as 1v2, declines — and is then barred
from the fight for twelve seconds, which is **twice as long as the average fight lasts**.

Fixed: the lock now only applies to a body that was actually in the fight.

| Seeds 5000–5199 | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Kills per match | 27.3 | 31.4 |
| Fights per match | 26.2 | 33.6 |
| Moments where a side lost 2+ | 2.21 | 2.77 |
| Biggest fight of the match | 5.7 | 5.9 |

### 3.3 The death clock — the one balance change that shipped

`combat.respawn_per_level_s` 1.4 → 2.2, so a level-18 death costs 47.6 s instead of 33 (real LoL is
~50 s at 18). One number, and it moved four things the right way at once:

| Seeds 5000–5199 | 1.4 | **2.2** | 3.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Macro roster win rate | 37.5% | **39.5%** | 34.0% |
| Kills per match | 31.4 | **28.4** | 27.6 |
| Gold-lead conversion | 68.0% | **64.0%** | 70.5% |
| Longest match in the batch (min) | 40.9 | **38.8** | 40.0 |

It paid back the kill overshoot from §3.2 exactly, brought conversion back onto target, nudged the
win vector, and shortened the worst-case game — which is the closest phase G got to the *decisive
endgame* item. 3.0 is worse on every count, so this is a peak and not a slope.

### 3.4 Measured and rejected

Four things were built or tried, measured over 200 sims each, and are **not** in the shipping
build. Each is recorded here so it is not re-tried by accident.

**Shortening the disengage lock further.** 12 s → 6 s → 3 s. Kills go 31.4 → 35.7 → 39.5, against
pro's 28 — and the share of fights that are two bodies does not move *at all*: 66% / 64% / 64%. The
dial buys kills, not fights.

**Lowering damage to lengthen fights.** `fight.damage_scale` 2.2 → 1.9 → 1.7 → 1.5. Fight duration
stayed at 6 s in every single arm. The reason is instructive: that 6 s is mostly the 5 s
`fight_end_grace_s` tail, so real *contact* is only one to three seconds. Fights are short because
bodies bounce in and out, not because anyone dies quickly, and no amount of damage tuning touches
that. Match length went *down*, not up.

**Scaling siege damage by the numbers instead of an on/off switch.** Today two defenders zero out
five attackers' tower damage entirely; the replacement had each defender tie up one attacker and
the rest swing freely. More faithful, and it is a real mechanical explanation for why a losing
nexus bleeds. But measured it does not deliver: the nexus-under-attack probe went 28.8 → 27.2
samples per match and the longest match in the batch 40.9 → 39.9, while the macro roster paid 3.5
points and blue-side drifted to 55%. Reverted.

**Holding a won fight at low HP** (`fight.hold_when_winning_edge`, built and **shipped gated off at
99**). A body below the disengage threshold stays in the fight anyway if it has a local body
advantage — because a real player at 40% in a 4v1 does not walk home. It works mechanically: low-HP
declines fall from 19% to 7%, moments where a side loses 2+ go 2.42 → 3.03, the biggest fight goes
5.8 → 6.1. But the two-body share barely moves (66% → 63%), kills overshoot to 31.9, and the macro
roster pays 2.5 points. The tunable stays in `data/balance.json` because this is a
drama-versus-fidelity trade and that is a designer call, not a technical one — see §5 q2.

## 4. The thing phase G could not fix

**Fights are small, and it is structural.** Every lever tried moves kills, match length or win rate;
none of them moves the share of fights that are two bodies. It sits at 63–66% across *sixteen*
measured arms.

The diagnosis, as far as the instrument can take it: a fight in this sim is one to three seconds of
contact. Bodies arrive, exchange, and break off, and the next exchange is scored as a *new* fight —
which is why fights per match went from 26 to 34 when the lockout was fixed while the fights
themselves stayed the same size. Five bodies never stand together because nobody stands anywhere
for more than three seconds.

That is a combat-engine question (how long a committed body stays committed), not a macro one, and
it is bigger than a balance pass. It is written up as the first item in §5.

## 5. Open questions for the designer

1. **Do you want big fights, and is it worth a combat-model change?** Today a match's biggest fight
   is ~5.8 bodies and 3% of all fights reach six; the rest are 2v2s. Making a real 5v5 happen means
   changing how long a body stays committed once it engages — a change to the combat model in GDD
   §6.1, not a number. It is the single biggest remaining gap between this sim and a pro game, and
   it is also the prerequisite the M6 close-up view is waiting on. **This is the one I would spend
   the next milestone on.**

2. **`fight.hold_when_winning_edge` — drama or fidelity?** Shipped off. Turning it to 1 gives you
   noticeably more multi-kill moments (2.4 → 3.0 per match) and slightly bigger fights, at the cost
   of kills overshooting pro (28.4 → 31.9) and ~2.5 points of the macro win vector. One number
   either way.

3. **Match length: 29.1 minutes against pro's 32.** Every lever tried this phase pushed it *down*.
   This is also the whole of the kills-per-minute miss — 28 kills is right, 29 minutes is not, so
   the game reads slightly frantic. The direct levers are tower and nexus HP, which nothing else in
   the sim depends on. Do you want me to spend them on length, or is a 29-minute match fine for a
   game the player watches at 1x?

4. **First tower at 7.4 min against pro's 10–12.** Turret plating (M5-F1) moved it from 5.6 to 7.4
   and helped the macro roster. More plating is available and would presumably keep helping. Left
   alone because it was not measured this phase.

5. **The playtest gate.** M5 is signed off on numbers. The remaining gate is you watching a match at
   1x and saying whether the plays read as plays. `tools/reel.gd --seed=N` gives you the match's
   moments as text first, which may be the faster way to decide where to look.
