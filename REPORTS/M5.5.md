## What to look at in-game

Run it and watch a fight at **1x**:

1. **Blows are traded.** Every auto-attack the sim fires now draws a beat — a projectile crossing
   to its target for a ranged character, a slash for a melee one, a spark where it lands.
2. **The catch reads as a catch.** When a CC lands, the victim wears a mark for exactly as long as
   the sim's lock lasts (icy ring + spurs for a slow, amber ring with stars orbiting the head for a
   stun/root/knockup) and a dashed tether ties it back to whoever cast it. Watch a jungle gank: the
   slow lands, the tether appears, the victim can't get home.
3. **Ultimates land.** One shape per effect family, bigger and louder than the same family from the
   basic-ability slot: shockwave at the ability's real radius for AoE, a beam for single-target
   (piercing for a snipe, a cross stamped on the victim for an execute), a bloom on everyone healed
   or shielded, an aura for a self-buff. The ability name now sits on a dark pill so it survives a
   busy fight.
4. **Sieges have a clock.** A turret shows a health bar once it has been chipped and flashes red
   under 35%; the nexus shows one when it starts taking hits. A dive under a low turret reads as
   the race it is.
5. **Characters, not dots.** One silhouette per role — broad hexagon top, arrowhead jungler, star
   mid, pentagon carry, octagon-and-cross support — team-tinted, turned toward its target or its
   path, with a facing pip. Bodies no longer stack: overlapping players are pushed apart for
   drawing, and handles in a crowd drop to their own line instead of printing over each other.
   Dead players fan out around the fountain instead of sitting on one grey dot.

Placeholder art contract is unchanged (CLAUDE.md): a character's `sprite` data field still
overrides the silhouette with a real texture, same tint and facing, no code change.

## What phase G changed (your playtest notes)

Your four observations were all real, and three of them had the *same* root cause, which is worth
stating plainly because it explains why v1 looked so nearly-right: **at 1x, playback runs four
times sim-time** (40 ticks a real second against the sim's 10). Every visual lifetime in the viewer
was written in sim ticks, so everything was on screen four times shorter than it read on paper — an
ult impact lasted **0.65 s**, an auto-attack beat **0.12 s**. You were catching glimpses of things
that were, in fact, only glimpses. Lifetimes are now sized in real seconds at 1x (ult impact 1.6 s,
its name 2 s, a swing 0.2 s) and they stretch with the speed button up to 4x.

**"The character blinks yellow but neither life moves."** Two separate bugs.
- The amber halo meant `in_combat`, which the sim sets on anyone who has *committed to a nearby
  enemy* — including two laners standing off across the wave, doing nothing to each other. Measured
  over three seeds: only **30% of in-combat player-frames are actual exchanges**. So the halo now
  means *trading blows* (a swing fired in the last second, while still committed) and is hot red, no
  longer competing with the amber CC ring. Backing off draws chevrons trailing behind instead — the
  sim breaks off fights constantly and that used to look identical to fighting.
- Life *was* moving, a couple of pixels at a time on a 22-pixel bar. Playback now diffs HP between
  snapshots and shows **floating damage numbers** plus a white hit-flash on the body. Every number is
  a real HP delta the sim produced — nothing is invented for show.

**"I do not see life of buildings."** The bar existed and was on screen for 89% of a match; it was
3 px tall, sitting on a lane line. Turrets now **drain like a battery** (the coloured core shrinks
with HP, so state is on the glyph and needs no bar to be read), the bar is wider and outlined, a
destroyed turret leaves rubble, and anything losing HP *this second* gets an orange siege pulse.

**"The game finished without any understanding how."** This one is half readability, half a design
finding you should know about.
- Readability: the side panel now always shows **turrets standing and nexus health for both sides**,
  the feed announces every turret ("Azure Wolves ⌂ top base turret", "Crimson Ravens nexus is exposed
  on top") and the nexus passing 75/50/25/10%, and the nexus diamond drains. The end of seed 45 now
  reads: *Dragon ▸ Azure Wolves · Azure Wolves ⌂ top base turret · Crimson Ravens nexus is exposed on
  top · nexus at 25% · nexus at 10%*.
- The finding: **the sim ends games by a slow minion grind.** Over 12 seeds the losing nexus bleeds
  for **6 minutes on average** (13 minutes in the worst case), often with no champion near it — the
  siege rule chips a structure whenever the lane front is pinned and the attacker has *any* minions
  there. Turrets do the same: several sit at 1–9 HP out of 3400 for minutes because the push stalls
  just short of killing them. So "no enemy hitting the nexus, and minions in front" was an accurate
  read of the screen. Nothing is broken, but a decisive endgame is a *balance* job: I've left it for
  M5-G rather than smuggling a pacing change into a readability milestone.

**Ultimates.** Beyond the retiming: the caster flares white-hot on cast (so an ult is visible even
when its impact lands across the fight), the name sits on its pill for 2 s, and each one gets a feed
line with its body count — "Cinder ult ▸ Sunlance Barrage · 1 hit". 54 ults in a 27-minute match, so
the feed absorbs it.

**On body collision** — you're right that what you now see is the fix (bodies are pushed apart for
drawing, so nothing overlaps) and that a macro-score shift could be tuned back. That's the answer I
needed: it stays a **viewer** layout pass for now, and giving bodies real volume in the sim goes on
the M5-E/F list as a *tunable lever* rather than a free win, since the numbers below show it pulls
several dials at once.

## What phase H changed (your 2026-07-26 note)

> "Nexus being destroyed alone or without clear vision is due to minions. I think animation of
> minions walking exist but in reality minions are hitting nexus."

You were right, and it turned out to be worse than a drawing problem — so this got fixed on both
sides of the line.

**The wave really was too far away.** An open lane (all its turrets gone) let the wave push to lane
param 0.06 / 0.94, and I measured what that is in map terms: **11 world units from the nexus on mid,
14 on top and bot** — an eighth of the map. The minions were draining a nexus from a screen-distance
away, so "no enemy hitting nexus (too far from it)" was not a misread; it was exactly what was
happening. The push bound is now `towers.open_lane_front` in `data/balance.json` (**0.015**, the base
doorstep, 6–7 units), so the wave walks into the base and grinds it there. It is a data value because
it is also a **balance dial** — how fast a won lane converts into a win — and it is the first half of
M5-G's "decisive endgame" item.

**And what is chipping a structure is now drawn chipping it.** A squad grinding a turret or a nexus
swings at it: each minion pokes on its own beat, so the clump reads as a crew at work rather than
dots standing still. Matching is by proximity to a structure that is *actually losing HP this
instant*, so nothing is drawn that the sim is not doing.

`--selftest` now fails if a structure is chipped with no besieging minion drawn, or if a nexus falls
with nothing drawn hitting it. That assertion caught the 14-unit gap in the first run — it reported
"a nexus fell with no minions drawn hitting it" before I had found the bound.

## The one real decision — separation is a *balance* question, not a rendering one

The backlog preferred a small **sim-side** body separation (bodies collide, which also spreads
combat naturally). I built it, measured it, and **did not ship it**. Same 200 sims, same seeds
(5000–5199), only difference being the separation pass:

| Metric | M5-D baseline | sim-side separation | target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Azure Wolves (macro) win** | 36.5% | **30.5%** | ~43% |
| **Blue-side win** | 49.5% | **43.5%** | ~50% |
| **Gold-lead @15 → win** | 66.3% | **71.1%** | ~65% |
| Match length avg (min) | 26.9 | 26.6 | 25–35 |
| Kills / match | 22.0 | 23.6 | |
| First blood (min) | 4.8 | 4.1 | |
| Gank connect rate | 16% | 22% | |
| First tower is bot | 10% | 27% | |
| Lane swap rate (team-games) | 24% | 34% | |

Giving bodies volume changes fights: it costs the macro team ~6 points, undoes M5-D's blue-side
fix, and pushes snowball past its target. Interesting — the connect rate and bot-tower numbers go
*up*, which is the kind of thing M5-E/F wants — but it is a **design change with balance
consequences**, and it has no business riding in on a readability milestone.

So separation ships as a **playback layout pass** instead: overlapping bodies are relaxed apart
for drawing only, splitting each overlap evenly so the group's centre of mass is preserved — the
cluster still sits exactly where the fight is. The sim is untouched and **provably** so (below).

**Answered (2026-07-25):** you're happy with the drawn separation, and treat the macro-score cost as
tunable. It therefore stays viewer-side, and sim-side body volume is carried into M5-E/F as a
deliberate lever to tune, not as a bug to fix.

## Sim-side additions (all playback-only)

Snapshot player rows are positional and append-only; playback reads combat state instead of
guessing it from the event stream:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 9 | target row (who this player is attacking, −1 for nobody) |
| 10 | combat flags: in-combat / disengaging / stunned / slowed / recalling |
| 11 | tick of the last auto-attack fired |

Plus `towers` (only *chipped* structures) and `nexus` on each snapshot, a `cc_applied` event
(source, victim, kind, until, slot), and `pos` / `radius` / `targets` on `ultimate_cast` and
`basic_cast`.
