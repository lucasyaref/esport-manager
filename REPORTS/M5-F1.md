## 2. Note 2 — the kill audit

I built the measurement before touching anything. The batch runner now reports **kills per minute of
game time** in five-minute bands — each divided by the minutes actually *played* in that band across
the batch, because a raw count makes late bands look empty simply because most matches have already
ended — plus **deaths by role** and a **nexus-defence probe**.

Then the pro numbers to compare against: **LCK 2025, 222 games — 28 kills a game over 32:10. LEC 2025
Spring, 106 games — 28 kills over 33:42.** Both regions land on **~0.85 combined kills per minute**.

Your sim, before this phase (200 sims):

| Minute band | Kills/min | Share of all kills |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | 0.26 | 6% |
| 5–10 | 0.38 | 8% |
| 10–15 | 0.55 | 12% |
| 15–20 | 1.43 | 31% |
| 20–25 | 1.32 | 25% |
| 25–30 | 1.46 | 15% |
| **whole game** | **0.84** | — |

**0.84 against pro's 0.85** — and the shape over the clock was right too: about six kills by the
15-minute mark, which is what a pro game does. So "not enough deaths" was a true observation with a
different cause than it looked like. Two things were genuinely off:

**(a) Your games were five minutes shorter than pro games** — 27.1 min against 32. At a correct
kills-per-minute a shorter game simply contains fewer kills: 23 instead of 28. Those are your five
missing kills. Two thirds closed (§6): matches now run **29.0 min and produce 26.3 kills**. Closing
the rest should be a deliberate lever, not a side effect — question 2.

**(b) Deaths are concentrated on two roles.** Per team per game:

| Role | Sim | Pro, roughly |
|---|---|---|
| top | 1.3 | ~2.8 |
| jungle | 1.35 | ~3.0 |
| **mid** | **3.95** | ~2.5 |
| **carry** | **3.4** | ~2.3 |
| support | 1.4 | ~3.3 |

In pro play deaths are close to flat across the five roles, and the support usually has the *most*.
Yours gives mid and carry three quarters of them. This is the same fact as your note 3, it is the one
real fidelity gap the audit found, and it is **not fixed** — §5 says why and what I now think it is.

## 3. Note 1 — the answer

> "At 7:25 the blue jungler pass behind the red mid on the midlane while his blue tower-1 is being
> attacked. Instead of attacking the red-mid to help his midlane he seems to go jungle bot. Does not
> look like human-pro decision."

Right, and the reason is structural rather than a bug: **every play the sim could make was one the
jungler chose** — a gank, a sandwich — and choices were rolled on a fifteen-second cadence. A real
jungler spends half his game on plays the **map forces on him**, and those need a much faster clock,
because a fight is over in ten seconds.

So there is now a third kind of call, the **answer**, checked every 2 s with no phase restriction, in
two shapes:

1. **A team-mate in a fight we are behind in** — outnumbered, or the man there nearly dead. An even
   1v1 trade in lane is deliberately *not* an answer; running at every trade is how a jungler ends a
   game with no camps cleared and no plays made. (The first cut did exactly that: 33 calls a match.)
2. **An enemy on a tower of ours that is genuinely under siege** — their wave pinned on our tower
   line, not merely an enemy laner standing near a turret. Here the bar is deliberately lower: your
   scene is a lone enemy mid hitting our T1 with our mid there, and at our own tower that even 1v1 is
   a free 2v1 and the play a pro jungler makes.

He walks *at* the spot rather than past it — no cut-off, unlike the sandwich, because the target is
already committed to something; being there is what is needed. The call goes on the blackboard so the
lane collapses with him, and the feed says **"Azure Wolves answers mid (1 follow)"**.

One deliberate departure: **the answer is not `macro`-gated.** Every other call in this sim is — a
sharp roster sees more of them. Answering a fight at your own turret is baseline professional
competence, not a macro edge, and I measured it both ways: the gated version cost the macro roster
several points for exactly the reason roams failed twice (the sharp team makes more plays, so it eats
more of the cost).

Tuning took four passes — 33 calls a match → 22 → 15 → **3.5**, which is where it reads right and
stops costing the jungler his map. Priced against an otherwise identical build over 400 sims, it is
worth **+4 points of gank connect rate** and costs about 4 points of macro win rate, which the rest of
the phase pays back.

## 4. Note 5 — the nexus, where the cause was not what it looked like

> "Blue team did not protect nexus and left it undefended even if minions are pushing. Nexus should be
> priority. Push the lane that are threatening (at least one player not necessarily all the team)."

I added a probe reporting, every 2 s that a nexus is taking damage, how many defenders stand on it.
Over 200 sims: **2.39 defenders on average, and only 15% of samples had nobody home.** They *were*
mostly coming back. What they could not do was anything about it.

The bug was two bugs. **Lane presence was a lane assignment, not a position** — only a player whose
`lane` matched *and* who was FARMING contributed to killing minions, so a player who had walked home
to defend was GROUPING and therefore, to the minion simulation, not there at all. And **players only
killed minions when two waves had collided**, so a wave walking at an undefended nexus, with no
counter-wave left, was literally invulnerable to the five players standing on it.

Both fixed: presence is **positional** (whoever stands at the front kills what is in front of them,
whatever they were doing), and it now applies to a wave pinned on your own tower line with no
counter-wave — the siege you walk home to break.

**And your second sentence is now the rule**: a base or nexus threat is worth the whole team, an outer
tower is worth the nearest `defense.defend_men` (3) while the rest keep farming. Measured cost:
**none** (42.0% with, 42.5% without — inside noise).

**What I did not ship, and want your call on.** Making defenders *visibly sweep* a wave off their
tower means a much bigger per-player clear rate, and that is not a free fidelity win: a lane in this
sim sits pinned on somebody's tower most of the game, so raising it is really "everybody pushes
harder". Each row is its own 200-sim run:

| defenders' clear rate | Azure (macro) win | gold-lead conversion | kills/match | length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **as shipped** | **34.5%** | **67.3%** | 26.3 | 29.0 min |
| ×2.7 | 38.0% | 71.0% | 28.4 | 30.2 min |
| ×6.7 | 29.0% | 73.0% | 29.8 | 31.5 min |
| ×4, no open-lane discount (first cut) | 3.3% | 92.9% | 29.1 | 30.4 min |
| _targets_ | _~43%_ | _~65%_ | _28 (pro)_ | _~32 (pro)_ |

Note it is **not monotonic** — I tried to use it as a balance dial and it is not one; lane equilibria
are threshold-y and small changes flip them. So I shipped the setting that changes lane pushing
*least*, and left the fidelity question to you rather than tuning a knob I do not trust.

## 5. Note 3 — the support, measured, and one rejected fix

Confirmed: the support dies **1.4 times a team-game** against the mid's 3.9. In pro play the support
is usually the most-killed role.

My hypothesis was target selection: the sim picks targets by threat, threat is built from damage and
gold, and the support has the least of both — so it was never anybody's target. I built the fix —
target scoring also weighs **killability**, with gold counting toward durability, since a support
earns barely half what the carry does and is the squishiest body on the map by the mid game.

**It didn't work.** 200 sims per setting, measuring the support's share of all deaths:

| `fight.squishy_bias` | Support share of deaths |
|---|---|
| 0 (off) | 12.3% |
| 2.0 | 12.4% |
| 4.0 | 13.0% |

So it is **gated off** — left wired, like M5-D's collapse, so M5-G only has to turn a dial if the real
cause is found. The measurement points elsewhere, and the kill-location table says it out loud: **43%
of kills happen in mid lane and 12% in top.** The support does not survive fights, it is not *in*
them. Whether that is the roam cadence (item 5, already queued for M5-F2), the peel positioning
holding it behind the carry, or bot lane simply mattering less than mid, I would want to measure
before building. It is a named M5-G item now instead of a guess.

One thing I can rule out: **it is not the healing.** Lumen's shield restores about 77 HP per ally at
level 6 — roughly 7% of a health bar. She never dies because nobody attacks her, not because she
out-heals them.

## 6. Note 6 — the early game, and why the first tower is the lever

Your kill numbers say the early-game *rate* is pro-accurate. What was not pro-accurate is that **the
first tower fell at 5.6 minutes.** In the real game turrets carry plating until 14:00, which is why a
pro first tower goes down around 10–12 minutes. A tower that dies to the first shove ends the laning
phase before it has produced anything to watch.

Towers now have **plating**: they take `towers.plating_reduction` (55%) less damage at minute zero,
easing to none by minute 14. One multiplier, two data values, no new machinery.

Measured on its own it is the one change in this phase that *helps* the macro roster (**+3 points**) —
a scaling team wants the game to last — and it is what moved first tower to 7.5 min, length to 29.0
and kills to 26.3.

A mechanism worth remembering, because it will keep recurring: **anything that raises the number of
fights favours the mechanical roster.** Crimson wins straight fights; Azure wins through towers,
objectives and time. That is why the answer costs points, why raising the wave-clear costs more, and
why plating — which buys time — pays. M5-G's balance pass should close the remaining 34 → 43 gap by
making macro *pay more* (objective value, the tempo payoff, comeback terms), not by making teams fight
less.

answers share the jungler's commitment clock, and the second seed set put gold-lead conversion at
73.5% against 67.3% on the first — that spread is wider than I would like and is worth a bigger batch
when the balance pass runs.

## 8. Questions for you

2. **Match length.** 29.0 min against pro's 32. Getting there is a deliberate lever (tower and nexus
   health) and would close the last of your missing kills, but it costs about a real minute per match
   at 1x, taking a watched match from ~8 to ~9 minutes. Worth it?
3. **The support.** Do you want it to *die* more (it should be in more fights — the roam cadence,
   item 5, is queued for M5-F2), or to *be* weaker (a durability change to Lumen in
   `data/characters.json` you can read and tune yourself)? Those are different games and I would
   rather build the one you mean.
