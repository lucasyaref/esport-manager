# M5-F2 — Coordinated multi-man plays, and the gank set-up that failed

Status: **done — the committed play shipped (+4 points, the largest gain in M5). The "laner sets up
for the gank" half was built, measured at −7.5 points, and reverted. Your 1x playtest is the gate.**

This phase covered your remaining two coordination items:

- **Item 3 — coordinated multi-man moves.** "The team brain dispatches 2–3 players to converge on
  one lane/objective, with a target, a committed set, and a window."
- **Item 1 — gank coordination that reads as communication.** Telegraph the gank earlier, and have
  the laner set up for it.

One shipped and is the best result this milestone has produced. The other is a design of yours that
the numbers contradict, so it is written up below as a question rather than quietly dropped.

---

## 1. The committed play (item 3) — shipped, +4 points

The pick this dispatches on has existed since M5-D: an enemy caught over-extended and *alone* on our
half, with enough of ours in reach. What never worked was the **answer** to it. M5-D expressed it as
`intent = "collapse"` — a team-wide yank that pulled all five off their lanes. Measured then, it
*lost* the macro roster points (it rolls the gate more often, so it over-commits more often), and it
has been gated to zero ever since.

The fix was not a better detector. It was a **committed set**:

- **a target, the bodies that agreed to come, and a window** (12 s). Everybody else carries on
  farming, sieging or holding their lane — the play costs two or three players, not five;
- **joining is rolled per player, against that player's own `macro`**, nearest asked first. The sharp
  roster arrives three-handed; the poor one sends nobody;
- **a play without enough bodies is never called** (`play_min_men` = 2). One player walking at an
  over-extended enemy is the 1v1 whiff that sank both earlier roam attempts — and it costs the farm
  either way. The commitment threshold is what makes leaving lane pay;
- **the set stays committed for the window** — the team's own intent cannot walk those players off
  mid-collapse, and the play never interrupts a recall, a camp clear, or a gank already in flight.

`defense.punish_base` / `punish_lift` are switched on for the first time, plus seven new
`defense.play_*` tunables. Code: `TeamBrain._consider_play`, `PlayerAgent.set_play`.

**This also settles items 2 and 5** (proactive roams, support mobility), which you moved here after
M5-E. The support roams when it is part of a committed play, never on its own dice — which is exactly
what the two reverted roam experiments (M5-C, M5-E2) said was missing. No separate roam mechanic was
needed or built.

**Why it works where more fighting usually doesn't.** M5-F1 established a rule that kept recurring:
*anything that raises the number of fights favours the mechanical roster* — Crimson wins straight
fights, Azure wins through towers and time. The committed play **breaks that rule**: it raises kills
per match (26.3 → 27.3) *and* gains the macro roster 4 points. A coordinated collapse is not a
straight fight — it is a fight whose numbers edge was already decided before it started, which is
precisely the thing macro is supposed to buy.

## 2. The gank set-up (item 1) — built, measured, reverted

The model, as written in GDD §6.1 after your 2026-07-26 direction: the laner *holds* the target while
the play is inbound — neither shoving it home to safety nor backing off — and commits when the
jungler lands. I built it as a two-stage call (`setup` → `commit`, a new lane stance) and it made
things clearly worse.

It also had a real bug on the first pass — `setup` fell through to the tower-safety pullback, so a
laner "setting up" actually walked *backwards* out of tower range, the exact opposite of the intent.
Fixing that did not rescue it: with the bug fixed it still cost 7.5 points.

**Why, and I think this is the useful part:** in this sim, **engaging early is what pins the
victim**. A laner that merely holds leaves the victim free to disengage the moment a third body
appears, so the gank arrives at a target that is already leaving. The old behaviour — all-in the
instant the call is heard — is effectively *start the fight now, the jungler joins it*, and a target
already committed to a fight cannot cleanly walk away from the third body.

This is the same principle the answer already encodes ("walk **at** the spot, not past it: the target
is already committed, so taking its escape route away is not what is needed — being there is"), and
the mirror image of why the sandwich's cut-off *does* work: there the victim is deep and the geometry
denies the retreat, so arriving behind it is the catch.

So the reactor all-ins on hearing the call, as before. The **telegraph half of item 1 already
existed** and is unchanged: the call posts when the jungler *commits*, not when it arrives, so the
lane has the whole walk to react. GDD §6.1 has been rewritten to match what is true.

## 4. What to look at in-game

At 1x, watch for **two or three players peeling off together** onto someone caught deep on their own
half — and, just as much, for the rest of the team *not* going. A play fires roughly 7–8 times a
match, about a quarter of them convert into a kill, and they read as the team deciding something
rather than five dots drifting to the same place. `play_call` events name the participants.

The Azure Wolves (the macro roster) should now be visibly better at this than the Crimson Ravens —
that difference *is* the win-vector, and it is the thing to judge.

## 5. Questions for you

1. **The gank set-up (§2).** A laner visibly setting up for an incoming gank — holding the target,
   then committing when the jungler lands — is more *readable* than a laner who simply all-ins on the
   call. It also costs 7.5 points of the macro win-vector and pushes the snowball to 71.5%. I shipped
   the version that plays better. If you want the readable version anyway, it is one revert away and
   I would want to spend M5-G finding the points back elsewhere.
2. **Play frequency.** 7–8 committed plays a match, ~2.25 bodies each. That is a lot of map movement
   — deliberately, and it is what the +4 points is made of. If it reads as *busy* rather than
   decisive when you watch it, `defense.play_interval_s` and `punish_base` are the two dials, and I
   would rather you judge it by eye than have me pick a number.
3. **Still open from M5-F1**, unchanged and still yours: the defence trade (do defenders visibly
   sweep waves, at a cost in macro win rate?), match length (29.5 vs pro's 32, ~1 extra real minute
   per watched match), and whether the support should *die* more or *be* weaker.
